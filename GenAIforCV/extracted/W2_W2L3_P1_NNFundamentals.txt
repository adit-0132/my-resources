Components of a Biological Neuron
A biological neuron consists of a cell body (soma), dendrites that receive inputs, and an axon that sends outputs. 

Neurons communicate through synapses, where the axon of one neuron connects to the dendrite of another neuron. 

Electrochemical signals travel from the dendrites through the cell body and along the axon, allowing information to be processed and transmitted throughout the nervous system.

Introduction to Neural Network
An Artificial Neural Network (ANN) is a computational model inspired by biological neural networks. Its basic processing unit is the artificial neuron, which forms the foundation of the network.

The basic unit of an ANN is an artificial neuron, which processes information through three operations: multiplication, summation, and activation.

Introduction to Neural Network
Input Level: Input values from the data are multiplied by their corresponding weights, which determine the importance of each input. 

Body Level: All weighted inputs are added together along with a bias value to produce a weighted sum. 

Output Level: The weighted sum is passed through an activation function to generate the final output of the neuron.

Applications of Neural Network
Data-Rich Problems
 Neural networks perform well in domains where large amounts of labeled or unlabeled data are available for training. 
Examples: Recommendation systems, sentiment analysis, and customer behavior prediction.
Pattern Recognition Tasks 
Neural networks are effective at identifying patterns, features, and regularities within large and complex datasets. 
Examples: Face recognition, handwritten digit recognition, and object detection.
Problems with No Explicit Algorithm
Neural networks can learn solutions for tasks where no clear set of rules exists.
Examples: Machine translation, emotion detection, and speech understanding.
Noisy or Uncertain Data Environments
 Neural networks can handle incomplete, noisy, or inconsistent data while still making reliable predictions. 
Examples: Sensor data analysis, medical diagnosis, and fault detection systems.
Non-Linear and Complex Problems 
Neural networks can effectively handle problems where the relationship between inputs and outputs is highly non-linear or mathematically ill-defined.  Examples: Image classification, speech recognition, and other pattern recognition tasks.

Linear Separability: A perceptron can classify data only if the classes are separated by a straight line or hyperplane.
Works for linearly separable problems. 
Cannot solve non-linearly separable problems such as XOR.

Single-Layer Model: The perceptron has only one layer and no hidden layers.
Simple and easy to implement. 
Limited in learning complex patterns and relationships.
