Autoregressive Generative Model

--- slide 4 ---
The model receives an initial input or prompt. It predicts the probability distribution of the next element/token.

One token is selected from this distribution. The selected token is added to the sequence.

The process repeats until the desired output is generated.

Example:
Input: “The cat is”
Model predicts: “sleeping”
 “The cat is sleeping”
 predicts the next token
 continues until the sentence is complete.
How Autoregressive Image Generation Works

--- slide 5 ---
How Autoregressive Image Generation Works
An image is converted into a sequence of pixels, patches, or tokens. 
The model observes the previously generated elements. 
It predicts the probability distribution of the next visual token. 
A token is selected and added to the sequence. 
The process continues until the complete image is generated.
Figure Source: Mengqi Huang, Zhendong Mao, Zhuowei Chen, Yongdong Zhang, “Towards Accurate Image Coding: Improved Autoregressive Image Generation with Dynamic Vector Quantization”, Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11718

--- slide 6 ---
Auto-regressive Models: Advantages, Limitations
Advantages:
Captures complex spatial relationships between image components.
Provides a clear probabilistic framework for image generation.
Can be conditioned on text, labels, or other images.
Can generate diverse and realistic visual content.

Limitations:
Sequential generation can be computationally expensive and slow.
Large images require many tokens.
High-quality generation may require substantial training data and computing resources.

Fully Visible Belief Network (FVBN)

--- slide 9 ---
FVBNs are typically trained using maximum likelihood estimation, where the objective is to maximize the probability assigned to the training images.

During generation, pixels or tokens are sampled sequentially according to the learned conditional distributions. 

Models such as PixelRNN and PixelCNN are prominent examples of this fully visible autoregressive approach. 

Although FVBNs provide an explicit and tractable likelihood function and can model complex image distributions, their sequential sampling process can make image generation computationally expensive and relatively slow.

PixelCNN
PixelCNN: A simplified, fully convolutional alternative of fifteen layers that preserves spatial resolution and uses masked convolutions to capture a bounded receptive field.

While generation remains sequential, PixelCNN allows for complete parallelization of the pixel computations during training and evaluation

Masks are adopted in the convolutions to avoid seeing the future context;

--- slide 14 ---
Masked convolution in PixelCNN is a modified convolution operation used to ensure that when predicting a pixel, the model only uses information from previously generated pixels and not from future pixels. 

In a standard convolution, the filter can access all neighboring pixels, which would violate the autoregressive property required for sequential image generation. 

To solve this, PixelCNN applies a mask to the convolutional filters that sets certain weights to zero, blocking connections to future pixels (such as pixels to the right or below the current pixel). 

This ensures that each pixel is predicted based only on pixels above and to the left.

--- slide 15 ---
PixelRNN
Pixel by pixel is generated.

The process is started in general from the left top corner.

An RNN or LSTM is used to model the dependency on previous pixels for the current pixel.

PixelRNN treats an image as a sequence of pixels and predicts each pixel based on the pixels that come before it.

It generates an image by generating one pixel at a time.

PixelRNN Architectures
Row LSTM: A unidirectional layer that processes the image row by row from top to bottom using a 1D convolution. 

This layer captures a roughly triangular context above the current pixel but is unable to capture the entire available context

Diagonal BiLSTM: Designed to capture the entire available global context. 

It skews the input map by offsetting each row by one position to parallelize calculations along diagonals, utilizing a highly nonlinear 2×1 state-to-state convolution.
