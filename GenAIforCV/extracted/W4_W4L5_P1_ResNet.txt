--- slide 5 ---
Activation Function are not enough to handle Vanishing Gradient Problem Completely
Leaky ReLU reduces but does not eliminate the vanishing gradient problem. For negative inputs, its gradient is small but non-zero (typically α < 1). During backpropagation through many layers, these gradients are repeatedly multiplied, causing them to decay exponentially. As a result, very deep networks can still experience vanishing gradients, although the effect is less severe than with standard ReLU, sigmoid, or tanh.
Why Leaky ReLU Does Not Completely Solve the Vanishing Gradient Problem ?
Small Negative Gradient: For negative inputs, Leaky ReLU uses a small slope α (e.g., 0.01). Although the gradient is non-zero, it is still less than 1, so repeated multiplication across many layers causes gradients to shrink. 
Primarily Addresses Dying ReLU: Leaky ReLU is designed to prevent neurons from becoming permanently inactive ("dying ReLU"), not to completely eliminate vanishing gradients in very deep networks. 
Cumulative Gradient Decay: Even without zero gradients, repeatedly multiplying gradients smaller than 1 during backpropagation causes them to decay exponentially, making training of very deep networks difficult.
To effectively address the vanishing gradient problem in very deep networks, architectures such as Residual Networks (ResNets) use residual (skip) connections that allow gradients to bypass one or more layers, preserving gradient flow and enabling the successful training of much deeper models.

--- slide 6 ---
ResNet Basics
ResNet (Residual Network) is a deep convolutional neural network architecture that addresses the degradation problem encountered when training very deep networks. 
It introduces residual learning through skip (shortcut) connections, which allow the input of a residual block to bypass one or more convolutional layers and be added directly to the block's output. 
Instead of learning a direct mapping, the network learns a residual function enabling more effective gradient propagation during backpropagation and mitigating the vanishing gradient problem. 
This design allows the successful training of networks with hundreds of layers, such as ResNet-50, ResNet-101, and ResNet-152. 
In computer vision applications, ResNet serves as a powerful feature extractor for tasks including image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, instance segmentation, and image retrieval. 
Its robust hierarchical feature representations, scalability, and compatibility with transfer learning have made it a foundational backbone in numerous state-of-the-art computer vision systems.

Introducing SKIP Connection
Instead of learning the complete mapping H(x), a ResNet learns the residual mapping:
		F(x)=H(x)−x

where F(x) represents the difference (residual) between the input and the desired output. Learning only what needs to change [F(x)] is often easier than learning the entire transformation from scratch [H(x)].

Advantages of SKIP Connections
[IMAGE: image.png.png 0.7x0.9in]

By providing a direct pathway for information and gradients to flow across layers, SKIP connections effectively mitigate the vanishing gradient problem, allowing stable training of very deep networks. 
Skip connections enable the network to learn identity mappings when additional layers are unnecessary, simplifying the optimization process and preventing performance degradation as network depth increases. 
They also accelerate training convergence by facilitating faster gradient propagation during backpropagation.
Furthermore, skip connections promote feature reuse by preserving useful low-level and intermediate representations throughout the network, leading to richer feature extraction. 
Their architecture provides implicit regularization, which can reduce overfitting and improve generalization. Collectively, these advantages make skip connections essential for training deeper, more accurate, and more robust neural networks for a wide range of computer vision applications.

Regularization effect :
Skip connections make the network behave like many shallower networks.
This improves generalization to unseen data.
As a result, it helps reduce overfitting.

Robustness to Network Depth : 
Skip connections allow additional layers without causing optimization difficulties. 
Reduces the risk of vanishing gradients and makes deep networks easier to optimize. 
Facilitates the design and training of very deep architectures. 
Adds no trainable parameters or complexity.
