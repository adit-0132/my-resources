## Errata and cross-chapter findings

(Appended during authoring.)

### Errata batch 1

- **The Week-2 decks use BOTH $\theta$ and $\phi$ simultaneously** — $\theta_{d0}, \theta_{di}$ for
  hidden-layer parameters, $\phi_{j0}, \phi_{jd}$ for output-layer parameters, bold $\boldsymbol{\phi}$
  for the whole collection. **"Translate $\phi \to \theta$" is NOT a clean substitution.** Lec 7 keeps
  the deck's per-parameter symbols where quoting the deck, uses $f(\mathbf{x};\theta)$ /
  $\mathbf{W}^{(l)}$ / $\mathbf{b}^{(l)}$ for general and matrix forms, and gives an explicit mapping
  table. **Lec 8 and Lec 9 have been messaged to match.**
- **`Week2.pdf` page 52 carries an unanswered in-deck exercise** — "How many parameters does this
  model have?" for a 3-input / 3-hidden / 2-output network, with no answer anywhere in the deck. Lec 7
  works it (answer **20**), cross-checked against the deck's own answered case (1–3–1 → 10 params,
  page 40), confirming $D(D_i{+}1) + D_o(D{+}1)$. **Add to the exercise table.**
- **`Week2.pdf` page 49's "5 / 10 / 20 linear regions" labels correspond to 4 / 9 / 19 hidden units** —
  an off-by-one trap. Regions $= H+1$ in 1-D.
- **`Week2.pdf` page 51** shows, without saying so, that multi-output networks' outputs **share all
  joint locations**. Lec 7 teaches it.
- **`Week2.pdf` page 36 is a recap of Lec 6's gradient-descent plot** sitting inside Lec 7's range.
- **`Week1.pdf` page 78 (Lec 3) and page 88 (Lec 4) share a worked example** — the
  $P = 0.3$ / $C(\text{question}) = 600$ / count $= 180$ setup. Lec 3 owns the MLE half; Lec 4 re-uses
  it with $|V| = 1210$ for add-1. No overlap, but both chapters cite the same numbers by design.
- **"Approximating Shakespeare" (`Week1.pdf` p. 74) belongs to Lec 3**, which owns the *qualitative*
  unigram→4-gram progression. Lec 4 owns *how* you sample (Shannon's method, pp. 104–116). No conflict.
- **`Week1.pdf` page 71 reverses this book's convention**: it writes the n-gram *order* as $N$ and the
  *position* as $n$. Flagged by Lec 3; any chapter quoting that page must do the same.
- **`Week1.pdf` pages 63 and 64 are byte-identical duplicates.**
- **`assets/figures/lecNN/` is largely unusable across this course** — most extracted images are the
  repeated NPTEL / IIT-KGP logo (identical ~73 KB files) or page decoration. **Default to Pool B
  (rendered pages)** and check Pool A only when you need a native-resolution chart or paper screenshot.
- **`extracted/Week2.txt` is nearly empty for Lec 7's range** (page 43 is literally just "1. compute
  three linear functions"). The Week-2 decks are plot-driven. Reading the rendered pages is not
  optional there.

### Errata batch 2 (Week 1–2 completion)

- **There is a FOURTH NLP paradigm.** `Week1.pdf` pp. 118–119 and 144–145 list **structured
  prediction** alongside classification / sequence labeling / generation, developed via **dependency
  parsing**. Lec 5 covers it as a fourth subsection. **No chapter in this map owns dependency parsing
  or its metrics (UAS/LAS)** — Lec 5 put them in "Beyond the slides". Genuine gap if parsing is examined.
- **The deck's confusion matrix is the TRANSPOSE of sklearn's** — Jurafsky's slides put system output
  on rows, gold on columns; `sklearn.metrics.confusion_matrix` is rows = true. This silently swaps
  precision and recall. Flagged by Lec 5; any chapter reusing metrics must match.
- **Micro-$F_1$ ≡ accuracy** for single-label multi-class. Not on the deck; Lec 5 states it as an identity.
- **`Week2.pdf` p. 87 writes the network scalar-by-scalar using a DIFFERENT Greek letter per layer**
  ($\theta$ layer 1, $\psi$ layer 2, $\phi'$ output), so an unprimed $\theta$ there means the *first
  hidden layer*, not "all parameters". Compounds the $\theta/\phi$ issue in Errata batch 1.
- **Two typos on the Lec 9 deck:** p. 102's Jacobian bottom-right entry is printed
  $\partial f_4/\partial a_4$ but $f$ has only 3 components (should be $\partial f_3/\partial a_4$);
  p. 104 prints the operator $\partial/\partial\omega_3$ where it is computing $\partial f_3/\partial\beta_3$.
- **The Lec 9 deck uses DENOMINATOR layout for Jacobians** — $\partial f/\partial a$ is
  (dim a)×(dim f), the transpose of the usual textbook convention. Never stated on the slides, and it
  is the reason $\partial f_3/\partial h_3 = \boldsymbol{\Omega}_3^\top$ rather than $\boldsymbol{\Omega}_3$.
- **The Lec 10 deck derives only He initialization, never Xavier**, and never states
  zero-initialization / symmetry breaking. Both added by Lec 10 and flagged as editorial.
- **Lec 10 deck notation clash:** $\boldsymbol{\beta}_k$ is the **bias vector** in the init section and
  $\beta$ is the **momentum decay rate** 20 pages later; Adam's second-moment decay is written $\gamma$,
  not $\beta_2$; the bias-correction exponent is $t{+}1$ because the loop index starts at $t=0$.
- **Correction to the standard Adam story, verified numerically by Lec 10:** the usual gloss is that
  uncorrected moments make early steps *too small*. The net effect at $t=1$ with default $\beta$s is the
  **opposite** — the uncorrected step is 3.162× too *large*, because $\mathbf{v}$'s bias factor
  $\sqrt{1-\beta_2} = 0.0316$ sits in the denominator under a square root against $\mathbf{m}$'s 0.1.
- **Momentum in the deck's $(1-\beta)$ convention is initially SLOWER than plain GD** on a
  well-conditioned bowl. Its real win is stability at learning rates past GD's divergence limit.
  PyTorch uses the other convention.
- **`Week2.pdf` pp. 99, 105, 106, 108 are the same slide four times** (different factor highlighted);
  pp. 92–97 are a six-step reveal of one backward pass.
- **`Week2.pdf` example network on p. 85 has exactly 43 parameters** — usable concrete figure.
