--- slide 10 ---
Bayes Theorem
P(A): Prior probability: The prior probability of event A, denoted by P(A), is the probability of A occurring before knowing whether event B has occurred or not. Here, A is treated as a proposition or hypothesis, and B represents evidence that may later be used to update the probability of A.
P(B) = Evidential probability: the probability pf the evidential event B.
P(A|B) = Posterior probability: The probability of proposition or hypothesis A post the occurrence of the evidential event B. 
P(B|A) = Likelihood: how likely the evidence B is, given that proposition A has occurred.

Probability and Random Variable
There are two types of random variables:
Discrete Random Variable – Takes a finite or countable number of values (e.g., the number of heads obtained when tossing three coins).
Continuous Random Variable – Takes any value within a given interval (e.g., the height or weight of a person).
